A Practical Workflow for Using AI Image Generators

Better image results usually come from a clear brief, deliberate prompt details, and careful review—not from adding endless adjectives.

Editorial draft · Test your chosen image tool and add original examples before publication.

1. Start with the purpose

Decide where the image will be used: a blog header, a product concept, a social post, or a presentation. The purpose determines the aspect ratio, composition, detail level, and whether space is needed for text.

2. Build a prompt from five parts

  1. Subject: What should appear?
  2. Setting: Where is the subject?
  3. Composition: Close-up, wide shot, overhead view, or another framing.
  4. Visual direction: Lighting, palette, materials, and mood.
  5. Output needs: Aspect ratio, empty space, and elements to avoid.
Example prompt structure:
“Create a clean editorial illustration of [subject] in [setting], framed as [composition], using [visual direction]. Leave clear space on the left for a headline. Avoid logos, watermarks, and unreadable text. Aspect ratio: 16:9.”

3. Generate, inspect, and refine

Review the first result for composition, subject consistency, unwanted objects, and any text errors. Change one or two prompt details at a time so you can see which instruction affects the output. Keep a note of prompts that produce useful results.

4. Check rights and privacy

Before commercial use, read the tool’s current terms for generated content, attribution, and commercial permissions. Avoid uploading confidential files or identifiable people’s images unless you have the necessary permission and understand the service’s data practices.

5. Prepare the image for the destination

Crop to the correct aspect ratio, check readability on mobile, compress the image sensibly, and write descriptive alt text for accessibility. If the image illustrates a real product feature, make sure it does not mislead readers into thinking it is a genuine screenshot.

Quick quality checklist

  • Does the image match the page’s purpose?
  • Is the main subject easy to identify at small sizes?
  • Are there unwanted marks, distorted details, or misleading elements?
  • Have you checked commercial-use and privacy terms?
  • Is the file optimized and the alt text accurate?

Conclusion

Use a repeatable prompt and review process. The best workflow is the one that reliably creates suitable results while meeting your quality, rights, privacy, and time requirements.
